The woman who sat in the owner's box at every Detroit Lions Thanksgiving game has died.
Martha Firestone Ford, owner and chair emeritus of the Lions, died on November 24, 2026, at her home, surrounded by her family.
She was 101. She was the granddaughter of the founder of the Firestone Tire and Rubber Company, and the widow of Henry Ford's grandson.
In the next minutes: how the Lions honored her on Thanksgiving, the day she took control of a losing team and fired the front office, and who owns the Lions.
The Lions announced her death on the evening of November 24, according to NFL.com.
The team's statement began: "It is with heavy hearts that we acknowledge the passing of Martha Firestone Ford."
It called her "a pillar of the Detroit community and the passionate matriarch of the Detroit Lions football team."
It said she would be remembered for "her fierce loyalty to her family, her community and the city of Detroit."
And it closed: "Her presence on our sideline, in our building, and in our community will be greatly missed."
No cause of death was given, WSB-TV reported, citing ESPN. In a separate post, the team said she "remained present with the team even this season."
At 101, she was still coming to games. And that is why the tribute planned for Thanksgiving meant so much in Detroit.
According to WSB-TV, Martha Ford attended every Lions Thanksgiving Day game, and the team said it would pay tribute to her when it hosted the Chicago Bears on Thanksgiving, November 26, 2026.
Detroit Sports Nation reported the details. A moment of silence before the game at Ford Field.
A helmet decal with her initials, "MFF," worn by every Lions player.
And a commemorative patch on the jerseys starting with the December 6, 2026, game in Atlanta.
So on the one day of the year when the whole country watches the Lions, her initials were on every helmet.
And this was not an ordinary Thanksgiving game. It was one of the biggest in years.
The Lions came into Thanksgiving at 8 and 2, according to the Chicago Sun-Times.
The Bears were also 8 and 2. The paper called it a battle for first place in the NFC North.
The Lions had won six straight. The Bears had won five straight.
The Sun-Times says it is only the second time since television began covering the game that both teams arrived on Thanksgiving with winning records, and the first time since 1991.
There is another story line. The Bears' head coach, Ben Johnson, used to be the Lions' offensive coordinator, calling plays for quarterback Jared Goff.
On that day he was trying to beat him. The Lions also have running back Jahmyr Gibbs, who the Sun-Times says leads the NFL in yards from scrimmage.
The halftime show was booked too: Wu-Tang Clan. And the Lions have a Thanksgiving problem: the Sun-Times says they had lost eight of their last nine Thanksgiving games.
But after she died, the most emotional words came from a rival owner.
Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, whose team also plays every Thanksgiving, spoke about her on television, according to EssentiallySports via Yahoo Sports.
"I'll never forget her," he said. "Eyes were teared up, seriously." He said "we're going to miss her," and he said she "attended most of the meetings that I've attended for the last 30 years."
NFL commissioner Roger Goodell and his wife Jane issued a statement too.
It began: "Martha was special." It said she "was committed to making the Lions, and the game of football, better."
Detroit Sports Nation reported that Lions great Barry Sanders also released a statement, and that the Red Wings honored her before a hockey game.
But the owners remembered something else about her: she was not a figurehead.
Her husband, William Clay Ford Senior, died in March 2014 at 88, and she took control of the team.
She was 88 years old herself. According to NFL.com, the Lions went 11 and 5 and made the playoffs in her first season as owner.
Then the next season started with one win and seven losses. She did not wait.
NFL.com reports she fired the team president, Tom Lewand, and the general manager, Martin Mayhew, in the middle of the season.
She later fired head coach Jim Caldwell, even though he had taken the team to the playoffs twice in four seasons.
With adviser Ernie Accorsi, she hired Bob Quinn from the New England Patriots front office as general manager.
Owners in their late eighties do not usually clean house. She did.
And that raises the question every Lions fan is asking.
The answer is: her family, and nothing changed at the time of this report.
She stepped down as principal owner in June 2020, according to NFL.com and Wikipedia, and handed control to her daughter, Sheila Ford Hamp.
She kept the title of owner and chair emeritus. She leaves four children: Sheila Ford Hamp, Martha Parke Morse, William Clay Ford Junior, and Elizabeth Ford Kontulis.
Fox News, citing the Lions, says she also leaves 14 grandchildren and 17 great-grandchildren, though People reported 17 grandchildren.
Fox says the Ford family "proudly carries forward the legacy" of Mr. and Mrs.
Ford. No outlet has reported any change to the team's ownership, and we will not guess about her estate.

The Ford family's ownership of the Lions began on a day every viewer of this channel remembers.
According to WSB-TV, citing The Athletic, William Clay Ford Senior bought the team on November 22, 1963, the day President John F.
Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas. Wikipedia puts the price at 4.5 million dollars.
At that point, the Lions had won their last NFL championship in 1957, and NFL.com notes they still have not won one since.
Martha Ford was 38 years old when her husband bought the team.
She would watch it for the next 63 years. And there is still a lot we do not know about the coming days.
Here is what has not been made public. No funeral or memorial date appears in any report we read.
The team has not given a cause of death. We do not know whether the NFL will honor her on other Thanksgiving broadcasts, or whether the Lions plan a public memorial at Ford Field.
We also do not know whether the family will speak publicly before the funeral, or whether Sheila Ford Hamp will address the team.
What we do know is where her story began, in a family whose name is still on millions of tires.
Martha Parke Firestone was born on September 16, 1925, in Cleveland, Ohio, according to Wikipedia and WSB-TV.
Her father was Harvey Firestone Junior. Her grandfather, Harvey Samuel Firestone, founded the Firestone Tire and Rubber Company.
She graduated from Vassar College in 1946. While she was there, Wikipedia says, she met William Clay Ford at a lunch in New York arranged by their mothers.
They married on June 21, 1947, in Akron, Ohio. The Akron Beacon Journal called it "the biggest society wedding in Akron's history."
The tire family and the car family were joined in one marriage.
The couple settled in Detroit in 1949, People reports. And Detroit is where she spent the rest of her life.
The Lions' words keep coming back to one idea: she was always there.
"Over more than six decades with the Detroit Lions, she was a loyal and constant fixture of the franchise," the team wrote.
Jerry Jones counted the meetings. Goodell called her special. And the players will carry her initials on their helmets.
For a city that has waited a very long time for a championship, she was the one person who saw every chapter of the Ford family era, from the first season to this one.
The team ended its statement by wishing the Ford and Firestone families "peace in this time of mourning."
And the tradition she never missed is older than almost anyone watching.
The Lions first played on Thanksgiving in 1934, against these same Chicago Bears, according to Detroit Sports Nation.
The Bears won that day, 19 to 16, and all 26,000 tickets sold out two weeks early.
The game went out on 94 radio stations across the country. One year later, the Lions beat the Bears 14 to 2, and Dutch Clark scored all 14 points.
In 1962, the Lions beat Vince Lombardi's Green Bay Packers 26 to 14 in what fans call the Thanksgiving Day Massacre.
In 1997, Barry Sanders ran for 167 yards in a 55 to 20 win over the Bears.
And in 2000, a young quarterback named Tom Brady made his NFL debut on Thanksgiving against Detroit.
Martha Ford was in Detroit for almost all of it.
So: Martha Firestone Ford died on November 24, 2026, at 101, at home with her family.
She took control at 88 and fired the front office when the team was losing.
Her daughter runs the team. And on Thanksgiving, every Lions player wore her initials.
