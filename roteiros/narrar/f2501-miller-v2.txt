The name of a Pearl Harbor hero is staying on an American aircraft carrier.
On November 20, 2026, a spokesperson for Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao said: "There is no intention to change the name of the USS Doris Miller."
That ended three months of reports that the Navy had weighed renaming the future carrier, possibly for President Trump.
Doris Miller was the Navy mess attendant who manned a machine gun on December 7, 1941.
In the next minutes: how close the name came to changing, the steel that goes down on December 5, 2026, and the medal Miller still has not received.
According to NBC Dallas-Fort Worth, citing USNI News, the Navy's statement came on November 20. theGrio reports CNN received the same wording.
On November 23, ABC News reported the Navy repeated it, attributing the statement to Navy spokesperson Meghan Stoltzfus.
Military Times quoted the same sentence from a spokesperson for the secretary of the Navy.
One sentence, no explanation, and no comment from the White House in any of the reports we read.
The ship is CVN-81, a Gerald R. Ford-class carrier being built in Newport News, Virginia.
To understand why a short statement made national news, you have to go back to August.
On August 20, 2026, CNN reported, citing sources, that the Navy was weighing renaming the carrier and that naming it after President Trump had been considered, according to Wikipedia and theGrio.
No decision had been made. The Department of Defense said at the time it had "nothing to announce at this time," Wikipedia reports.
The Navy told Military Times it had "no announcement to share at this time."
Wikipedia also reports that in August, Trump sent a memo to the acting Navy secretary about removing the ship's electromagnetic catapults; that memo did not mention the ship's name.
For three months, the family waited. And when the answer came, they spoke first.
Thomas Bledsoe, Miller's great-nephew and the family's spokesperson, told CNN in August that the possibility of renaming the ship "was quite a shock," Military Times reports.
After the Navy's statement, the family said, according to NBC, "we remain encouraged by these reports of the ship maintaining its name Doris Miller."
Bledsoe added: "Doris Miller's legacy is not just our family's history; it is American history."
NBC reports the family has been invited to the keel-laying ceremony in December 2026.
ABC reports the statement came over the weekend of November 21. For the family, the name was the first fight.
The ship itself is the next story, and it is enormous.
According to Wikipedia, the USS Doris Miller is the fourth ship of the Gerald R.
Ford class. It will be 1,106 feet long and displace about 100,000 tons fully loaded, with a crew of about 4,660.
It is being built by Newport News Shipbuilding, part of Huntington Ingalls Industries.
The planned launch is October 2029. Military Times reports commissioning is expected in February 2034, after delays it attributes to constraints at the shipyard, and that the ship will take about 15 years to complete.
It will be the first American carrier named for an African American, and the first named for an enlisted sailor.
And the first ceremonial steel of that ship goes down on December 5.
Military Times reports the ceremonial keel laying is set for December 5, 2026, in Newport News.
Wikipedia says it had originally been planned for January 2026. The keel laying is when the sponsors' initials are welded into a steel plate that stays with the ship for its whole life.
The ship's sponsors are Charlene Austin and Taya Miller, according to Wikipedia.
The date matters: December 5 is just two days before the 85th anniversary of Pearl Harbor.
Miller's name will go into the steel that same week. But the family and supporters say a name on a ship is not the end of it.
There is still the medal.
According to Wikipedia, in August ABC News reported Miller was under consideration for an upgrade from the Navy Cross to the Medal of Honor, and one source said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had sent Congress a memo recommending it.
As of the start of September, no Medal of Honor had been announced.
Military Times reported on November 23: "As of this reporting, there has been no update on the push."
Military Times also reports that Maryland Representative Kweisi Mfume introduced legislation in March 2025 to award Miller the Medal of Honor and a Congressional Gold Medal, saying he wanted Miller "finally be recognized with an American salute."
So the ship is settled. The medal is not. And the reason goes back to one morning in Hawaii.
For the men who served in that war, this was never just a name on a ship.
December 7, 2026, marks 85 years since the attack on Pearl Harbor.
Many of you grew up with fathers and uncles who were there, or who served in the Pacific afterward.
The carrier will sail into the 2030s and beyond carrying the name of an enlisted sailor from Waco, Texas, who was 22 years old that morning.
That is how the Navy chose to remember him when it named the ship at Pearl Harbor.
And whether it will also give him the nation's highest honor is the question still open in Washington.

Doris Miller was born on October 12, 1919, in Waco, Texas, according to Wikipedia.
He enlisted in Dallas in September 1939 as a mess attendant, one of the few jobs open to Black sailors in the Navy then.
On the morning of December 7, 1941, he was aboard the battleship USS West Virginia at Pearl Harbor.
According to Wikipedia, he carried wounded sailors to safety and helped move the ship's mortally wounded captain, Mervyn Bennion, to a sheltered spot.
Then, with no training on it, he manned a .50-caliber Browning machine gun and fired at the attacking Japanese planes.
The Navy officially credited him with one plane. The next question is what the Navy gave him for it.
At the ceremony on December 5, the ship will exist as little more than a block of steel.
But the tradition is old. The sponsors' initials are welded in, and from that day the ship is officially under construction.
NBC reports Miller's family will be there. The Navy's statement means the name painted on the hull in the 2030s will be his.
It also settles one more thing: the carrier will be the first in American history named for an enlisted sailor.
Whether the medal follows, nobody in Washington has said. The first time Washington had to decide about Doris Miller, the answer was no.
In 1942, after newspapers and civil rights groups pressed for recognition, according to Wikipedia, members of Congress introduced bills to give Miller the Medal of Honor.
Military Times reports that Representative Carl Vinson, the Georgia Democrat who chaired the House Naval Affairs Committee, argued Miller's actions did not merit it, and Navy Secretary Frank Knox agreed.
Instead, President Roosevelt approved the Navy Cross on May 11, 1942. Admiral Chester Nimitz pinned it on Miller on May 27, 1942, aboard the carrier USS Enterprise at Pearl Harbor.
Wikipedia says he was the first Black recipient of the Navy Cross.
That is the medal the family hopes to see upgraded.
Here is what has changed. Back then, the Navy's leadership said no.
In 2026, according to the reporting Wikipedia cites, the Defense Secretary himself has reportedly recommended the upgrade to Congress, and a bill is pending in the House.
Wikipedia notes that no Black sailor, soldier or Marine received the Medal of Honor for actions during the Second World War while the war was on; the first, Vernon Baker, received it decades later.
So the Navy had settled the name in Miller's favor. The open question is whether Congress and the White House will settle the medal too.
Miller himself never saw the end of the war.
Doris Miller was later assigned to the escort carrier USS Liscome Bay.
On November 24, 1943, during the Battle of Makin in the Gilbert Islands, a Japanese submarine torpedoed the ship, according to Wikipedia.
It sank in 23 minutes. Only 272 men of a crew of more than 900 survived.
Doris Miller was not among them. He was 24 years old. The Navy declared him dead a year later.
November 24, 2026, marked 83 years since he went down with that ship, and in the same days the Navy said his name will sail again.
Here is where we are. The Navy says it has no intention of renaming the USS Doris Miller.
The family says it is encouraged. The keel goes down December 5 in Newport News.
And the Medal of Honor upgrade is still waiting, with no update reported.
