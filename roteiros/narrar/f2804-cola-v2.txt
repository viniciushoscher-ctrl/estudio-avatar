The letter that tells you exactly how much Social Security will pay you in 2027 was already out in late November 2026.
According to Newsmax, citing the Social Security Administration, the one-page notice for the 2027 raise showed up in online accounts in late November, with paper copies by mail in December.
The raise is 3.6 percent. For the average retired worker, that is about 75 dollars more a month.
And Medicare Part B goes to 209 dollars and 40 cents. In the next minutes: what the notice actually shows, how much of the raise Medicare takes back, and the one thing that had not happened in four years.
According to Newsmax, people who use a my Social Security account could see their cost-of-living notice online starting in late November, up to three weeks before the paper notice.
The agency laid out that schedule in its October 14, 2026 announcement, Newsmax says.
If you have not signed up for paperless notices, a paper letter arrives by mail in December.
The notice, Newsmax reports, gives the exact dates and dollar amounts of your new benefit and of any deductions. 24/7 Wall St. describes three numbers on it: the new gross benefit, the Medicare deduction, and the net payment that actually lands in your bank.
That third number is the one that matters. So how big is it?
According to Newsmax, citing The Hill, retirement benefits rise about 75 dollars a month on average, or roughly 900 dollars a year.
The average monthly check for a retired worker becomes 2,170 dollars starting in January 2027.
That is an average; your own raise is 3.6 percent of your own benefit, which is why the notice matters more than the headline.
The Motley Fool reports that the average disabled worker gets about 59 dollars more a month.
Newsmax says about 72 million Social Security beneficiaries see the increase in January, and Commissioner Frank Bisignano put the total at 76 million Americans.
"Giving them greater financial stability," he said, according to Newsmax. But the check you see is not the check you keep.
Medicare comes first.
On November 17, 2026, the federal Medicare agency announced the 2027 premiums, 24/7 Wall St. reports.
According to Newsmax and The Motley Fool, the standard Part B premium rises 6 dollars and 50 cents, to 209 dollars and 40 cents a month.
In 2026 it was 202 dollars and 90 cents. For most retirees, that premium comes straight out of the Social Security check.
So for an average retired worker with a 75 dollar raise and a 6 dollar 50 premium increase, the arithmetic leaves about 68 dollars and 50 cents.
Newsmax notes that most people paying the standard premium will still see their net check rise by 3 percent or more.
That sounds small. But for 2027, it is actually good news, and here is why.
The Motley Fool calls it a rare bonus. It is not a separate payment.
The Part B increase works out to about 3.2 percent, according to the Fool, while the raise is 3.6 percent.
That means, for the first time since 2023, the Medicare premium rises by a smaller percentage than Social Security.
The Fool says roughly 35 million people on original Medicare benefit. Look at 2026 to see the difference: the raise was 2.8 percent, and the Part B premium jumped 9.7 percent, according to the Fool.
For 2027, the raise wins, by a small margin, but it wins.
For a retiree who watched the premium eat most of the 2026 raise, that matters.
But there are more dates on the calendar than January.
For about 7.3 million people on Supplemental Security Income, the increased payment begins on December 31, 2026, according to Newsmax.
Medicare's annual enrollment period ends on December 7, Newsmax reports, the main window to change a Medicare drug plan for 2027.
And drug plans change too. According to Newsmax, the Part D out-of-pocket cap becomes 2,400 dollars in 2027, and the highest deductible a plan can charge rises to 700 dollars.
Newsmax adds that your 2027 Medicare premium appears in the my Social Security message center in late November.
So why is the raise bigger than the one before it in the first place?
The raise is set by a formula, 24/7 Wall St. explains: the average of the July, August and September consumer price index for wage earners, called CPI-W, compared with the same months a year earlier.
Newsmax, citing The Hill, says inflation reached 3.7 percent in September 2026.
The Motley Fool attributes the bigger number to tariffs and the war with Iran, citing New York Fed economists who estimated tariffs had raised goods inflation by about 2.9 percentage points by February. 24/7 Wall St. says the "Trump bump" label on this raise reflects timing, not a policy decision.
Either way, a bigger raise means prices went up first. And some retirement writers say that is nothing to celebrate.
Christy Bieber, writing for The Motley Fool on November 28, 2026, put it bluntly: "every senior should be disappointed by the cost-of-living adjustment for a simple reason."
Her reason: a bigger raise is a sign of worse inflation. She also argues that the CPI-W may underestimate what seniors actually pay, because it gives less weight to health care. 24/7 Wall St. makes the same point, citing a research index for Americans 62 and older that weights housing and medical care more heavily.
Newsmax adds the bottom line: most retirees will not keep the full 3.6 percent after Medicare deductions.
To see whether 3.6 percent is big or small, you have to look back.

According to 24/7 Wall St., the raises of the last five years were 8.7 percent in 2023, 3.2 percent in 2024, 2.5 percent in 2025, 2.8 percent in 2026, and 3.6 percent for 2027.
Newsmax calls it the largest since 2023. The Motley Fool says it ties for the fifth-largest raise since 1993.
And Part B, according to the Fool, rose 5.9 percent in 2024 and 5.9 percent again in 2025.
For three straight years, the premium outran the raise. That streak ends in January 2027.
So how does all of this end up in one letter with your name on it?
The timeline matters, because each step is a different agency. The Social Security Administration announced the 3.6 percent raise on October 14, 2026, according to the Fool and Newsmax.
Medicare announced the 209 dollar 40 premium on November 17. Then the two numbers are combined into one notice per person, which is why the letter arrives weeks after the headlines.
Newsmax says about 55 million retired workers are among those getting the raise.
Each of them gets a notice with their own numbers, not the averages you hear on the news.
And the average hides a tax rule that hits some retirees harder than others.
According to 24/7 Wall St., Social Security benefits become taxable once your combined income passes 25,000 dollars if you are single, or 32,000 dollars for a married couple filing jointly.
Above a second level, up to 85 percent of your benefits can be taxed.
Those thresholds, the site notes, have never been adjusted for inflation. So every raise can push more retirees over the line, and a raise that looks like 75 dollars on the notice can be worth less after taxes in April.
That is worth checking against your notice. And it explains why a raise is never quite what it seems, a lesson retirees have learned before.
The Motley Fool reports that the CPI-W has been used to set the raise since 1975, and that there has been a raise every year since then except three: 2010, 2011 and 2016.
In those years, retirees got no raise at all. Three Januaries opened with the same check as the year before.
The Fool's Christy Bieber cites The Senior Citizens League's research that benefits have lost buying power over the past decade.
So a 3.6 percent raise is real money, but it is catching up, not getting ahead.
Which brings us to what to do with your notice.
Based on what the Social Security Administration told Newsmax, the steps are simple.
If you have a my Social Security account, look for the one-page notice and your 2027 Medicare premium in the message center.
Compare the gross benefit, the Medicare deduction and the net payment, the three numbers 24/7 Wall St. describes.
If you do not use the online account, watch your mail in December.
And if you want a different drug plan, Medicare's enrollment window closes on December 7.
After that, the next dates are December 31 for SSI and January for everyone else.
