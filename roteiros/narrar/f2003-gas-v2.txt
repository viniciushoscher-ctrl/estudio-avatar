The national average for a gallon of regular gas is down to 3 dollars and 72 cents, according to AAA.
That is the lowest price since the middle of March. Prices fell 18 cents in one week and 57 cents in one month, MLive and WRNJ report, citing AAA.
CNN calls it the biggest three-week drop since 2008. And it is happening right before Thanksgiving, when tens of millions of Americans get in the car.
In the next minutes: why prices are falling so fast, the Thanksgiving record AAA says we may avoid, and how much more you are still paying than before the war with Iran.
On November 19, 2026, the AAA national average was 3 dollars and 72 cents.
By November 20, it had slipped again, to 3 dollars and 69 cents, AAA's own daily figures show.
A week earlier it was 3 dollars and 91 cents. A month earlier, 4 dollars and 30 cents, WRNJ reports.
The average was below 4 dollars in 40 states, according to MLive.
GasBuddy's Patrick De Haan said on November 18 that prices had hit their lowest point since March 15, MLive reports.
Since November 3, the average is down 43 cents, or about 10 percent, Newsweek reports.
And prices fell in all 50 states and Washington, D.C., in the week before, according to El Cronista.
So the drop is everywhere. But not everywhere starts from the same place.
The most expensive state is Hawaii, at about 5 dollars and 60 cents a gallon, followed by California at 5 dollars and 51 cents, Nevada at 4 dollars and 80 cents, Washington at 4 dollars and 74 cents, and Oregon at about 4 dollars and 36 cents, according to AAA figures reported by WRNJ.
The cheapest is North Carolina, at about 3 dollars and 5 cents.
Then Indiana at about 3 dollars and 16 cents, Texas at about 3 dollars and 20 cents, Oklahoma at 3 dollars and 24 cents, and Ohio and Georgia at 3 dollars and 25 cents.
In the Northeast, Pennsylvania averages 4 dollars and 15 cents and New York 4 dollars and 7 cents, WRNJ reports.
In San Antonio, prices fell below 3 dollars a gallon for the first time since March, KSAT reported.
Hawaii is the only state where gas costs more than it did on Labor Day, Newsweek reports.
So why is the rest of the country getting relief?
There are three reasons. First, oil. Oil traffic out of the Persian Gulf has recovered close to prewar levels, according to the International Energy Agency, as reported by Newsweek.
"The oil market is showing tentative signs of rebalancing," the IEA wrote.
The agency expects global oil output to exceed demand by 1.7 million barrels a day this quarter, the first surplus since the war began, El Cronista reports.
U.S. crude oil, known as WTI, fell 2 dollars and 75 cents on November 18 to settle at 83 dollars and 26 cents a barrel, WRNJ reports.
It is down nearly 20 dollars a barrel since late October, according to El Cronista.
Second, the season: refiners switch to cheaper winter-blend gasoline, and people drive less in the fall, AAA says.
Third, supply: U.S. gasoline stocks rose to 215.9 million barrels, according to the Energy Information Administration.
U.S. crude oil inventories rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million, still about 3 percent below the five-year average, the EIA reports via WRNJ.
And the timing raised a question in Washington.
High gas prices became one of the biggest political issues heading into the November 2026 midterm elections, Newsweek reports.
On November 17, President Trump questioned why "they" waited until after the election to bring prices down, according to Newsweek.
GasBuddy and other experts dismissed the idea that the election had anything to do with it, pointing instead to the season and to global oil markets, Newsweek reports.
Prices actually peaked in 2026 at 4 dollars and 56 cents on May 21, and hit 4 dollars and 48 cents again in late September.
Whatever the politics, drivers care about one thing: what they will pay on Thanksgiving.
AAA wrote in a blog post on November 19 that it was possible the national average would drop another 15 cents by Thanksgiving and avoid setting a new record, according to MLive.
Thanksgiving Day is November 26. The current record for Thanksgiving Day is 3 dollars and 58 cents a gallon, AAA says.
On November 19, the national average needed to fall about 14 cents in one week to come in under it.
By November 20, the gap was down to about 11 cents. At that pace, 18 cents in a week, it was within reach, but not guaranteed.
AAA projects nearly 73 million people will take road trips over the holiday, MLive and WRNJ report.
El Cronista, citing AAA, puts total Thanksgiving travelers at 81.5 million, nearly nine in ten of them by car.
And many of them are still paying a price they never paid before the war.
On February 27, the day before the United States and Israel struck Iran, the national average was 2 dollars and 98 cents, El Cronista reports.
The strikes disrupted tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. Even after this drop, gas is still about 27 percent higher than before the war, which is nearly 12 dollars more for a 15-gallon tank, according to El Cronista.
In November 2025, the national average was 3 dollars and 9 cents, WRNJ reports, so drivers are still paying more than 60 cents a gallon above a year earlier.
El Cronista reports that this is still the most expensive November on record.
Utah has seen the biggest rise since February, 1 dollar and 26 cents, followed by Hawaii and Nevada.

Diesel fell to 5 dollars and 98 cents a gallon on Tuesday, November 17, its first reading below 6 dollars since early September, El Cronista reports.
By November 20, AAA had it at 5 dollars and 90 cents.
An analyst at Energy Aspects, Yigit Dogan, said falling wholesale prices should bring some relief at the pump, though diesel remains above prewar levels, according to El Cronista.
Diesel matters even if you never fill a diesel tank. It moves the trucks that carry food to the grocery store and the packages to your door.
When diesel stays high, prices on the shelf follow. When it falls, that relief arrives slowly, weeks later.
There is one upside: Bloomberg reports, as cited by Newsweek, that high diesel prices give refiners a strong reason to keep running, and the gasoline they make along the way adds to supply.
And this is not the first time Americans have watched the pump swing this hard.
The Thanksgiving record of 3 dollars and 58 cents was set on November 24, 2022, AAA says.
That fall, prices were coming down after the summer spike that followed Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, when the national average topped 5 dollars a gallon for the first time.
A war far away, a spike at home, and then a long slide.
Sound familiar? 2026 followed almost the same pattern, with a different war.
So the question is whether this slide lasts, or turns around again.
For drivers who have switched to electric, the national average for public charging held steady at 42 cents per kilowatt-hour, according to AAA figures reported by WRNJ.
Hawaii is the most expensive at 55 cents, and Kansas the cheapest at 32 cents.
Most older drivers still buy gasoline, but the gap is worth knowing if you are thinking about your next car.
Gasoline demand actually rose slightly in the latest weekly report, to 8.79 million barrels a day, while production fell, the EIA reports.
Supply still grew, because imports and stocks filled the gap. And that brings us to the biggest drop in years.
CNN reports this is the biggest three-week drop in gas prices since 2008.
That year, many viewers will remember, gas hit record highs in the summer, above 4 dollars a gallon, and then collapsed in the fall as the financial crisis hit and the economy fell into recession.
The 2026 drop has a different cause: oil flowing again after a war, not a crash in the economy.
That is the good version of falling prices. Since Labor Day, when the average was 4 dollars and 15 cents, prices have dropped about 10 percent, AAA says, according to Newsweek.
Montana has seen the biggest drop since September, 84 cents, followed by North Carolina at 78 cents and Washington at 77 cents.
The smallest drops were in West Virginia, 12 cents, Pennsylvania, 15 cents, and Nevada, 17 cents, Newsweek reports.
Around Labor Day, California prices reached as high as 5 dollars and 86 cents.
So here is where it stands. Gas is 3 dollars and 72 cents, the lowest since March, down 57 cents in a month.
AAA says it could fall another 15 cents by Thanksgiving and avoid the record of 3 dollars and 58 cents.
Watch the Strait of Hormuz and the oil price. If tankers keep moving, the IEA's surplus could keep pushing prices down into winter.
If fighting flares again, the slide could stop overnight. If you are driving across state lines for the holiday, check the average for each state on your route: the gap between the cheapest and most expensive states is more than 2 dollars and 50 cents a gallon.
AAA will post the Thanksgiving Day number on November 26. We will report it.
