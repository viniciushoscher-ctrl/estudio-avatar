Thanksgiving dinner for 10 people will cost 58 dollars and 63 cents in 2026, according to the American Farm Bureau Federation's annual survey, released on November 19.
That is 3 dollars and 45 cents more than in 2025, up 6.3 percent, Fox reports.
It is the first increase in 4 years. The biggest reason sits in the middle of the table: the average 16-pound frozen turkey is up 10 percent, to 23 dollars and 68 cents.
But the same bird is selling for less than half the average price at some stores.
In the next minutes: what went up, what went down, and where a meal for 10 costs under 40 dollars.
The Farm Bureau's survey prices a classic meal for 10: turkey, stuffing, sweet potatoes, rolls, peas, cranberries, a veggie tray, pumpkin pie, whipped cream and milk.
In 2026 the total came to 58 dollars and 63 cents, Fox reports.
That works out to about 5 dollars and 86 cents a person.
It is still 8 percent below the record set in 2022, when the same dinner cost 64 dollars and 5 cents.
So prices are not at the peak. But after 3 years of small declines, the line has turned up again.
And one item drove most of it.
The average 16-pound frozen turkey costs 23 dollars and 68 cents in the Farm Bureau's survey, up 10 percent, Fox reports.
Texas A&M's AgriLife Today, as reported by KOMO News, expects turkey prices about 8 percent higher than in 2025, with the national average for a frozen whole turkey around 1 dollar and 26 cents a pound, compared with 1 dollar and 3 cents in 2025.
AgriLife points to higher wholesale costs, the lingering effects of bird flu, and higher transportation and feed costs.
NielsenIQ data cited by the Latin Times shows turkey breast up 12 percent.
Fresh turkey runs about 11 percent higher per pound, Fox reports. But not every store is charging the average.
KOMO News checked store prices for frozen turkey by the pound. Target: 79 cents.
Walmart: about 84 cents. Aldi: 99 cents. Safeway: 1 dollar and 99 cents.
That means the same frozen bird can cost more than twice as much depending on where you shop.
On a 16-pound turkey, 79 cents a pound is about 12 dollars and 64 cents.
At 1 dollar and 99 cents, it is about 31 dollars and 84 cents.
That is a difference of more than 19 dollars for the same dinner.
Store prices change by the day, so check the ad before you drive.
And some stores are going further than a cheap turkey.
Aldi is offering a complete meal basket that feeds 10, turkey and sides included, for under 39 dollars, KOMO reports.
The Latin Times reports Kroger has a feast for 10 with store brands for under 50 dollars, and Sam's Club offers a meal for up to 16 people for about 160 dollars.
Walmart and Aldi meal bundles work out to about 4 dollars a person, according to the Latin Times.
Compare that with the Farm Bureau's 5 dollars and 86 cents a person.
KOMO also reports that choosing store brands for canned pumpkin, stuffing mix and broth can save 15 dollars or more.
So who says the meal costs even more than 58 dollars?
Not everyone is cooking for 10. Many older couples will sit down with 2, or 4 with a neighbor.
Using the Farm Bureau's average of about 5 dollars and 86 cents a person, a dinner for 2 comes to about 11 dollars and 73 cents.
At the roughly 4 dollars a person of the Walmart and Aldi bundles, it is about 8 dollars.
The catch is the turkey: a 16-pound bird feeds far more than 2, and at the Farm Bureau's 23 dollars and 68 cents, it alone would cost twice the rest of the meal.
That is why the per-pound price matters so much for small households.
A turkey breast is one option, but NielsenIQ data shows turkey breast up 12 percent, so it is not always the bargain it looks like.
For a couple, the cheapest whole bird at 79 cents a pound may still beat a small cut, if there is freezer room for leftovers.
And there is another way to count the bill.
The Wells Fargo Agri-Food Institute prices a bigger basket and gets a different number: 78 dollars and 93 cents for 10 people, Fox reports.
But by its count, that is actually down 0.4 percent from 2025.
Wells Fargo says 6 of the 10 items it tracks are cheaper than in 2025.
Its report says "there are no major price shocks on the plate," and that "nothing on the Thanksgiving plate is up by double digits."
Pumpkin pie is up 2.5 percent in its basket, and gravy mix is up 2.2 percent.
The biggest piece of the Wells Fargo bill is not food at all.
Beverages make up 48 dollars and 61 cents of the Wells Fargo basket, or 38.1 percent of the total, Fox reports.
Sparkling water alone is up 6.4 percent. That is a reminder for anyone hosting: the drinks can cost almost as much as the food.
Meanwhile, the accounting firm PwC estimates the average family Thanksgiving dinner will run about 90 dollars in 2026, up from 88 dollars in 2025, according to the Latin Times.
With extras like ham, alcohol or name brands, KOMO puts a household's bill at 80 to 100 dollars.
So which items are really climbing?
According to the Farm Bureau survey as reported by Fox, prices rose for boneless ham, milk, dinner rolls, sweet potatoes, russet potatoes, frozen peas, frozen green beans and cubed stuffing.
NielsenIQ figures in the Latin Times show cranberry sauce up 15 percent and fruit juice up 6 percent.
Green beans, carrots, soft drinks, dinner sausage and appetizers are each up about 4 percent.
KOMO reports the biggest increases are in fresh produce and cranberries. For a retiree on a fixed income, 4 percent here and 15 percent there adds up fast, especially when the Social Security raise is fixed for the year.
But there is good news on 2 items.

Butter is down 10 percent and cream is down 6 percent, according to figures cited by PwC's Ali Furman and reported by the Latin Times.
KOMO also lists butter and cream among the items that got cheaper.
For anyone making mashed potatoes, pie crust or gravy from scratch, that helps.
And the Farm Bureau's own frozen-turkey price per pound for birds over 16 pounds was about 96 cents, "virtually unchanged," Fox reports.
Furman said: "Families are managing their budgets very closely this year." NielsenIQ found that 77 percent of hosts are reworking their shopping lists instead of cutting back, the Latin Times reports.
So what are people doing differently?
Some stores are still giving turkeys away. KOMO reports that BJ's offered members a free Butterball for a single purchase of at least 150 dollars between October 31 and November 9, and that ShopRite offers a free turkey with a spending threshold.
The Latin Times reports the cash-back app Ibotta is giving new users up to 22 dollars and 99 cents back on qualifying purchases, including whole turkeys, through November 23.
Consumer expert Andrea Woroch told CBS News that combining store loyalty programs can save up to 25 percent, according to the Latin Times.
Those deals expire fast. And one more factor is changing what Americans buy.
About 1 in 8 American adults take a GLP-1 drug, the class that includes popular weight-loss shots, according to the Wells Fargo report cited by Fox.
Wells Fargo says 11 percent take one for weight loss, up from 3 percent in 2024.
A study it cites found grocery spending drops 5.3 percent within 6 months of starting one of these drugs.
For Thanksgiving, that may mean smaller portions and smaller birds in some homes.
It is one more reason the grocery business is watching this holiday closely.
And the 2022 record still hangs over it.
In 2022, the Farm Bureau's Thanksgiving dinner hit 64 dollars and 5 cents, the most expensive on record, as Fox notes.
Since then, prices drifted down for 3 years. The 2026 survey breaks that streak.
The 2026 meal is still about 5 dollars and 42 cents cheaper than that peak.
But for many households, the total on the receipt is not the whole story: it is what you paid compared with what your check went up.
For most retirees, the yearly Social Security raise is set once a year, while the grocery bill changes every week.
So here is where it stands. The Farm Bureau says Thanksgiving for 10 costs 58 dollars and 63 cents, up 6.3 percent, with turkey up 10 percent.
But frozen turkey ranges from 79 cents a pound at Target to 1 dollar and 99 cents at Safeway, per KOMO.
Thanksgiving is November 26, 2026, and Black Friday follows on November 27, the Latin Times reports.
Stores usually deepen turkey discounts in the last days before the holiday, as the Wells Fargo report notes that its prices reflect "the typical discounts leading up to Thanksgiving."
If you have not bought your bird, compare 2 or 3 store ads before the holiday, and buy the frozen turkey early enough to thaw it in the refrigerator.
Where it stands: the meal costs more, and the store you pick matters more than ever.
