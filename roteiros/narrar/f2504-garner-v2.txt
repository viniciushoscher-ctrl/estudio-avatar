The man who played the drums on one of the biggest records of the disco era has died.
Henry Garner Jr., the original drummer of the R&B group Rose Royce, has died at 72, the band announced on social media.
Reports appeared on November 25, 2026. Garner was part of the group behind "Car Wash," the song that went to number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and sold two million copies.
In the next minutes: what the band said, the movie that made that song famous, and who from the original lineup is still playing.
The news came from the band itself. According to MassLive, Rose Royce posted on Instagram: "Henry passed away today and we are all in tears."
The post ended: "RIP Henry you will be missed and never forgotten."
MassLive describes him as the original drummer of the legendary R&B group.
The band did not give a cause of death or say where he died, and none of the reports we read included those details.
The same report ran on cleveland.com, in The Oregonian and on NJ.com.
Tributes from other musicians quickly appeared on Facebook. To understand why so many people stopped to read the news, you have to hear a few seconds of hand claps.
"Car Wash" opens with hand claps and a groove that many of you can still hum.
According to Wikipedia, the single reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for one week in late January 1977.
It knocked Stevie Wonder's "I Wish" off the top, and was replaced by Mary MacGregor's "Torn Between Two Lovers."
It also hit number one on the soul chart, number one in Canada, and number nine in Britain.
On Billboard's ranking for the whole year, it finished at number 26.
The record was certified platinum, and Wikipedia says it sold two million copies.
The single credits list Henry Garner on drums. But the song was written for something bigger than a record.
Rose Royce came out of the backup bands of Watts and Inglewood in Los Angeles, according to Wikipedia.
The musicians first played together as Total Concept Unlimited, touring behind Motown singer Edwin Starr, who introduced them to producer Norman Whitfield.
Whitfield, the former Motown producer, signed them to his own label. The original lineup had nine members, Wikipedia reports: singer Gwen Dickey, Henry Garner on drums, Terry Santiel on congas, Duke Jobe on bass, Michael Moore on saxophone, Kenny Copeland and Freddie Dunn on trumpet, Kenji Brown on guitar, and Victor Nix on keyboards.
Their new name was chosen for one job: a Hollywood soundtrack.
The film was "Car Wash," from Universal Pictures, directed by Michael Schultz.
According to Wikipedia, it starred Richard Pryor as the preacher Daddy Rich, George Carlin as a taxi driver, Franklyn Ajaye, Ivan Dixon and Antonio Fargas, with Bill Duke, Garrett Morris and Professor Irwin Corey, and the Pointer Sisters singing a number backed by Rose Royce.
The screenplay was written by Joel Schumacher, who later became a major Hollywood director.
The movie cost about two million dollars and made about 14 million at the box office, according to Wikipedia's figures.
At the Cannes Film Festival, it won a technical grand prize, and Whitfield won the award for best music.
The band was renamed Rose Royce for the soundtrack, partly as a nod to Rolls-Royce.
And the soundtrack did even better than the film.
The "Car Wash" soundtrack won the Grammy for best score soundtrack album, according to Wikipedia.
It produced three R&B top-ten hits, including "I Wanna Get Next to You," which reached number ten on the Hot 100, and "I'm Going Down."
The theme was nominated for a Golden Globe for best original song.
Then came "Wishing on a Star" and "Ooh Boy," and later "I'm in Love (And I Love the Feeling)," another top-five R&B hit, according to Wikipedia.
Wikipedia reports "Love Don't Live Here Anymore" reached the top five on the Billboard R&B chart and number two in Britain, and Madonna later recorded it twice.
These are the songs Garner's drums carried onto radios all over the country.
So what happens to the band now?
Wikipedia's lineup of Rose Royce still lists Henry Garner, alongside original members Kenny Copeland, Kenji Brown, Duke Jobe, Victor Nix, Freddie Dunn, Michael Moore and Terry Santiel.
Singer Gwen Dickey left the group and later led her own act, Gwen Dickey's Rose Royce, which toured Britain and headlined the London Palladium, according to Wikipedia.
In the spring of 2010, when TV One made an episode of its documentary series "Unsung" about the band, Garner was one of only five members interviewed, along with Dickey, Copeland, Jobe and Moore.
The band had not announced what comes next for its shows at the time of this report.
And the songs he played on are still earning money.
Wikipedia reports that Mary J. Blige covered "I'm Going Down," The Cover Girls had a top-ten single with "Wishing on a Star," and Daft Punk sampled the band's "First Come, First Serve."
NBC announced a television revival of "Car Wash," according to Wikipedia. "Car Wash" itself was certified gold in Canada and New Zealand and silver in Britain, on top of the American platinum record.
Every cover, sample and movie airing keeps the music in circulation. The reports we read do not say how royalties were split among band members, so we will not guess.
What we do know is that the band's statement was the only official word at the time of this report.

Think back. The movie "Car Wash" had opened on September 3, 1976, according to Wikipedia.
By the last week of January 1977, the song was the number-one record in America.
Roger Ebert gave the film three and a half stars out of four.
Gene Siskel gave it three. Wikipedia reports the film holds an 86 percent score from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, and it built a cult following that its first run never had.
A year later, NBC put the movie on Monday Night at the Movies.
For many families, that was the first time they saw Richard Pryor's preacher on the living room set.
And behind every scene was a groove recorded by a band most viewers could not have named.

Until then, the band's own words are the ones that matter: he will be missed and never forgotten.
His story, like the band's, started long before the movie.
In 1973, according to Wikipedia, the musicians who became Rose Royce toured England and Japan as the backing band for Edwin Starr, the singer of "War."
Starr introduced them to Norman Whitfield, the producer behind many Temptations hits.
Whitfield signed them to his label. For a while they worked under the name Magic Wand, backing singer Yvonne Fair and serving as the studio and concert band for The Undisputed Truth.
It was the leader of that group who first spotted Gwen Dickey in Miami.
Within three years, the backing band was the main act.
Drummers rarely get the spotlight. In Rose Royce, the singer and the horn players stood in front, and the drummer sat at the back of the stage.
But the "Car Wash" single credits list Garner on drums, and the band's statement made clear what he meant to them.
He stayed with the group across the decades, which is why he still appears in its lineup.
His death leaves the surviving original members to carry the songs forward.
The band's history after its peak shows how hard that can be.
Gwen Dickey left in April 1980, and the group temporarily disbanded, according to Wikipedia.
Ricci Benson became the new singer from the album "Golden Touch" in 1980 through "Fresh Cut" in 1986, and Lisa Taylor sang on "Perfect Lover" in 1989.
Along the way came the albums "Strikes Again" and "Rainbow Connection IV," whose single "Is It Love You're After?" became one of the band's biggest hits in Britain.
The band kept recording and touring through the changes. "Car Wash" never left the radio.
And in November 2026, one of the men who played on it was gone.
