The guitarist who gave the biggest band of the MTV years its edge has died.
Andy Taylor, the original lead guitarist of Duran Duran, died peacefully at home on November 23, 2026, surrounded by his family.
He was 65. The cause, according to a joint statement from his family and his former bandmates, was prostate cancer, after what they called a long and courageous battle.
In the next minutes: the words his wife and his bandmates chose, the night he could not attend his own Hall of Fame induction, and the band he formed with Robert Palmer that cracked the American top ten.
The news came on November 25, on the band's official website. The statement said Andy Taylor "passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday 23rd November."
It said he died "following a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer."
It was signed by the four men he started with: "With love from Simon, John, Nick and Roger."
That is Simon Le Bon, John Taylor, Nick Rhodes and Roger Taylor.
None of the three Taylors in the band were related, a detail fans have joked about for decades.
Fox News Digital confirmed the death the same day, and Euronews followed on November 26.
The statement did not say where the family home is, and none of the reports we read gave a city.
The band wrote: "Andy was there at the very beginning of our story."
But the most personal words came from someone else.
His wife, Tracey, wrote on behalf of the family. "To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather."
She added: "He was the rock of our family." And she closed with: "We will miss him every single day."
According to the band's site and Euronews, he leaves Tracey and four children: Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew.
The band's site also names a grandson. The couple married in 1982, Wikipedia says, right as the band was taking off.
In lieu of flowers, the family asked for donations to Prostate Cancer UK, the charity that fights the disease that took him.
And the length of that fight is the part many fans did not know.
Andy Taylor was diagnosed with stage four metastatic prostate cancer in 2018, according to Wikipedia, which cites the letter he later wrote to fans.
Stage four means the cancer had already spread beyond the prostate. For years he kept the diagnosis private.
One report on his death describes it as an eight-year battle, and the dates line up: diagnosis, then eight more years of life, most of it out of public view.
He kept working. He made music, he produced records for other bands, and he went back into the studio with Duran Duran.
His bandmates have said little about his treatment, and we will not guess at it.
What we know is how the world found out. It was not a press release.
It was a letter, read out loud by his singer, on one of the biggest nights of the band's life.
In 2022, Duran Duran was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.
Andy Taylor was too ill to attend, the band's statement says. So Simon Le Bon read part of a letter from him on stage.
In it, Taylor revealed the stage four diagnosis. According to Wikipedia, which cites Variety, the letter said the condition was incurable but not immediately life-threatening.
Euronews quotes one line from that letter: "I would like to thank each of my brothers in this great band."
Think about that moment. The guitarist who helped build the band could not walk onto the stage where the band was being honored.
His friend read his words instead. That night, many fans learned for the first time that he was sick.
And it explains why the tributes sound the way they do.
Four of the original five are still alive: Simon Le Bon, John Taylor, Nick Rhodes and Roger Taylor.
Each wrote his own tribute on the band's site. Le Bon wrote: "There's a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart."
Nick Rhodes wrote him a poem, Fox News reports, calling him "an adventurer who found his new home."
Roger Taylor, the drummer, called him "the live wire from Newcastle." Andy had left the band more than once over the years.
But even while he was sick, he came back. According to Wikipedia, he rejoined the others in the studio to record the album Danse Macabre, released in November 2023.
So his guitar is on one of the band's most recent records.
The band had not announced any change to its plans at the time of this report, and the statement does not mention a memorial concert.
But if you want to know why his name still matters, you have to hear the songs.
The band's statement names the songs he helped drive: "Hungry Like The Wolf," "Rio," "The Wild Boys" and "A View To A Kill."
Fox News names "Rio" and "Hungry Like the Wolf" as the hits that made the band famous.
"A View To A Kill" was the theme song of a James Bond movie.
Those were the songs that played on American radio and on a brand-new cable channel that ran music videos all day.
If you had teenagers in the house in those years, you heard them through the bedroom door whether you wanted to or not.
Andy Taylor's job was to give the polished pop of the band a rock edge.
That edge is what made the music work on a car radio as well as on a television screen.
And he would soon prove he could play harder rock than anyone expected.

In 1985, two things happened that put Andy Taylor in front of almost everyone.
First, Duran Duran played Live Aid, the giant famine-relief concert, and Taylor was on stage with them, according to Wikipedia.
Second, he formed a side band called The Power Station, with bassist John Taylor, singer Robert Palmer and drummer Tony Thompson.
According to Wikipedia, their debut album reached the top ten in the United States.
Their single "Some Like It Hot" climbed to number six on the American chart.
Their cover of "Get It On (Bang a Gong)" reached number nine.
That was a hard rock record, with loud drums and loud guitar, and it sounded nothing like the pop the teenagers knew.
It was the kind of music older rock fans could respect. But some of what fans most want to know has not been made public.
Here is what has not been made public. No funeral or memorial date has been announced in any report we read.
The statement does not say where he died beyond "at home." It does not say whether the band will play a tribute at its next show.
And in the reports we read, no musician outside the band had yet issued a public tribute, though more are likely.
We also do not know what happens to his share of the band's catalog and royalties.
That is a private family matter, and no outlet has reported on it.
We will not guess. What we can tell you is where the story began, and it began with a long train ride.
Andy Taylor was born in Tynemouth, in the north east of England, on February 16, 1961, according to Wikipedia.
He grew up in the seaside town of Cullercoats. He started playing guitar at 11, and he took lessons from Dave Black, a guitarist with the Spiders from Mars, the band that backed David Bowie.
In April 1980, he answered an ad that a young band in Birmingham had placed in the music paper Melody Maker, Euronews reports.
He was 19. The band's statement remembered it: "From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East," he brought energy and fire.
That young band was Duran Duran. And his bandmates still remember that arrival in very personal ways.
Roger Taylor wrote that Andy "arrived on a train from Cullercoats." John Taylor remembered him coming from Whitley Bay, a town next door.
The details differ, the way memories of something that happened when you were 19 do.
But the feeling in every tribute is the same. They describe a loud, funny, fearless young man who walked into a room of art-school kids and turned up the volume.
Le Bon's line about the hole in his heart is the one fans are sharing most.
Nick Rhodes wrote a poem. And the four signed the joint statement together, as a band, the same way they signed their records.
But Andy Taylor's mark on American radio does not stop at Duran Duran.
After the first split, Taylor went to work for other legends. According to Wikipedia, his solo single "Take It Easy," from the 1986 movie American Anthem, reached number 24 in the United States.
His solo album Thunder followed in 1987. Then he co-wrote and co-produced Rod Stewart's album Out of Order, released in 1988, with Bernard Edwards and Tony Thompson.
That album gave Stewart American hits including "Lost in You," "Forever Young" and "My Heart Can't Tell You No."
Wikipedia also lists Taylor on lead guitar for Robert Palmer's "Addicted to Love."
So if you never owned a Duran Duran record, there is still a good chance you sang along to something Andy Taylor played on or produced.
The original five later reunited, and the album Astronaut came out in 2004.
He left again in 2006.
