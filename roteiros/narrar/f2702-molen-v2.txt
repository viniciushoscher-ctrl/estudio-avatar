The man who stood next to Steven Spielberg when Schindler's List won Best Picture has died.
Gerald R. Molen, known to friends as Jerry, was 91. His great-niece announced his death on Instagram, and Variety reported it on November 26, 2026.
Molen shared the Oscar for Schindler's List, produced Jurassic Park, and even appeared in it on screen.
Before that, he helped make Rain Man. In the next minutes: how a kid from a studio-lot cafe became one of the most powerful producers in Hollywood, the dinosaur movie where you saw his face, and the turn his career took late in life.
The news came from his great-niece, Rachel Lorraine Pullan, according to Variety.
She wrote that he had "passed on to join my great-grandma and his dear wife Pat."
She also wrote: "Uncle Jerry was a man of great moral character and high integrity."
Variety did not report the date, the place or the cause of death, and none of the reports we read included those details.
Pat, his wife of almost 70 years, had died before him, Variety reports.
No funeral or memorial plans have been made public. But his great-niece's post also told the part of his story that most moviegoers never knew: he did not start at the top.
According to Variety, Molen was born in Montana and moved to North Hollywood in 1947.
His parents, Gerald Senior and Edith, ran a cafe next to the Republic Pictures lot in Studio City, Variety says.
Wikipedia tells it slightly differently, saying his mother ran a diner called The Blue Onion across from a major studio.
Either way, the boy grew up next door to the movie business.
His great-niece wrote that he began "moving set pieces and driving at a movie studio," and that he "worked his way up to the position of producer."
Wikipedia lists his early credits behind the scenes: transportation coordinator on Absence of Malice, and unit production manager on Tootsie, with Dustin Hoffman.
That job is about trucks, schedules and money. And it put him in the room with the most powerful director of his generation.
Wikipedia gives his full name as Gerald Robert Molen, born on January 6 in Great Falls, Montana.
His parents were Gerald Richard Molen and Edith Lorraine Molen, and he was the oldest of several children.
Wikipedia says he grew up in North Hollywood after the family left Montana, a few miles from the big studio lots of the San Fernando Valley.
By his great-niece's account, he learned the business from the bottom, moving sets and driving, long before anyone gave him a title.
Before he ever produced a movie, Wikipedia lists him as unit production manager on A Soldier's Story, the Army murder mystery with Denzel Washington.
Unit production managers run the budget and the schedule on the ground.
It is the least glamorous job on a film. And it was the job that got him noticed by the biggest name in Hollywood.
Schindler's List, Steven Spielberg's film about the Holocaust, won Best Picture. Variety reports that Molen shared that Oscar with Spielberg and Branko Lustig.
Variety dates the award to the ceremony in 1994. Think about that night: a man who started out driving on a studio lot walked onto the Academy Awards stage as a producer of the best picture of the year.
Molen produced it, Wikipedia says, alongside a completely different kind of movie from the same director in the same year.
And in that other movie, you can see him.
Molen produced Jurassic Park, according to Wikipedia and Variety. He also stepped in front of the camera.
Wikipedia says he played Dr. Harding, the park's veterinarian, the man caring for the sick dinosaur when the visitors arrive.
So if you watched that movie with your children or grandchildren, you have seen the producer's face without knowing it.
He went on to produce the sequel, The Lost World: Jurassic Park, both sources say.
Wikipedia lists more small acting parts, in Rain Man, Days of Thunder, Amistad and Catch Me If You Can.
He was a producer who liked to be on the set. And his partnership with Spielberg ran deep.
Variety says the working relationship with Spielberg began on The Color Purple, where Molen was unit production manager.
That led to an associate producer role on *batteries not included, with Spielberg as executive producer.
Then the credits grew. According to Wikipedia and Variety, Molen produced Hook, with Dustin Hoffman and Robin Williams, and Minority Report, with Tom Cruise.
Wikipedia lists him as executive producer of Casper, Twister, The Trigger Effect and The Flintstones, and producer of The Little Rascals.
Variety adds Little Giants, the football comedy, to his Amblin films. Amblin is Spielberg's company, and for years Molen was one of the men who turned its ideas into finished movies on time.
Those are movies that filled theaters and video stores for a decade.
And some of his biggest hits were not with Spielberg at all.
Wikipedia credits Molen as co-producer and unit production manager on Rain Man, the movie where Tom Cruise drives across the country with his brother, played by Dustin Hoffman.
Variety lists it among his producer credits. He also appears in it briefly.
Then came Days of Thunder, the NASCAR movie with Tom Cruise, where Wikipedia lists him as executive producer and actor.
For a man who started out driving trucks on a studio lot, it is fitting that two of his films were about cars and the open road.
By now, he was one of the few producers trusted with the biggest budgets in town.

In 1993, two movies with Gerald Molen's name on them came out in the same year, according to Wikipedia: Jurassic Park and Schindler's List.
One was a summer adventure about dinosaurs. The other was a film about the Holocaust.
Few producers in Hollywood history have had a year like that. One movie packed theaters with families.
The other won Best Picture at the ceremony in 1994. If you went to the movies that year, there is a very good chance you paid to see his work twice.
And yet his name was never a household name. That is the life of a producer.

Wikipedia says that in his later years he split his time between Montana and Las Vegas.

Later in life, Molen turned to political documentaries. According to Wikipedia, he produced 2016: Obama's America, which it describes as a documentary critical of President Obama, released during the 2012 campaign.
He produced America: Imagine the World Without Her in 2014, and Hillary's America in 2016.
Wikipedia reports that critics panned Hillary's America and that it won four Golden Raspberry awards, including Worst Picture, the first documentary to do so.
He also produced Death of a Nation in 2018. Wikipedia describes him as a conservative Republican.
Whatever you think of those films, he made them knowing they would cost him friends in Hollywood.
And his beliefs went beyond politics.
Wikipedia describes Molen as an active member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.
He produced and acted in The Other Side of Heaven, a film about a young missionary, according to Wikipedia.
His wife, Pat, died in January 2024 at 86, Variety reports, after almost 70 years of marriage.
That is why his great-niece wrote that he had gone to join "his dear wife Pat."
For a man who worked in one of the most unstable businesses in the world, his marriage lasted longer than most Hollywood studios.
And he did not stop working.
Wikipedia lists Molen as executive producer of The Meg, the shark movie with Jason Statham, in 2018.
Variety also lists Meg 2, Just Like Heaven and The Best Christmas Pageant Ever among his credits.
According to Wikipedia, a company announced in 2019 that he would produce a limited series called Sweet By and By.
Wikipedia says he began preparations for that series in 2021. So, well into his eighties, he was still putting his name on projects, still reading scripts and still raising money.
The man who once drove trucks on a studio lot never really left the set.
