Bill Belichick, the coach with 6 Super Bowl titles in New England, is a finalist for the Pro Football Hall of Fame again.
Fox Sports reports he was moved forward on November 19, 2026, by the Hall's coach committee, and Patriots owner Robert Kraft was moved forward by the contributor committee.
Both were finalists in February 2026, and both were turned down. Each needs at least 80 percent of the full selection committee to get in.
In the next minutes: why the voters said no last time, how the Hall changed its rules because of it, and the date when we find out.
The Boston Globe reports that Belichick was the coach committee's choice and Kraft the contributor committee's choice, each picked from 5 semifinalists.
They join the players on the final ballot. At the Hall's annual meeting before Super Bowl 61, the selectors will cut 15 finalists to 10, then to 7, and then vote yes or no on 6 of the 7, the Globe reports.
Any candidate with at least 80 percent approval is elected, Fox Sports reports.
The class has no fixed size: the Hall's bylaws allow between 3 and 7 new members.
For Belichick, 80 percent was the number he did not reach last time.
In February, the Hall announced its Class of 2026: Drew Brees, Larry Fitzgerald, Adam Vinatieri, Luke Kuechly and Roger Craig, NBC News reported.
Bill Belichick was not on the list in his first year of eligibility.
Neither was Kraft. NBC reported that Belichick and Kraft were in a separate group of 5 from which voters could pick only 3.
ESPN had reported weeks earlier that Belichick did not get enough votes, citing, according to NBC, "his involvement in cheating scandals, such as Spygate and Deflategate."
The Boston Globe says his omission "generated considerable outrage." And the outrage did something unusual.
In September, 7 months after the snub, the Hall announced changes to its selection process, Fox Sports reports.
The selection committee was cut from 50 members to 25, plus 3 alternates, for a total of 28.
That means Belichick's fate rests with half as many voters as before.
Under the 80 percent rule, a small group can still block him.
Fox Sports reports that the Hall did not set a fixed number of inductees.
A smaller room, the same 80 percent bar, and a coach who has been told no once.
Some of the 2026 voters explained why they said no.
One voter, Mike Chappell of FOX 59 and CBS 4 in Indiana, cited Spygate and chose Kraft over Belichick, NBC News reported.
Spygate was the controversy in which Belichick and the Patriots were disciplined for illegally videotaping opponents' sideline signals.
Another voter, Vahe Gregorian of the Kansas City Star, wrote that he felt "duty-bound" to vote for senior players such as Roger Craig, who had a shorter window to get in, NBC reported.
So the no votes were not all about cheating. Some were about math: only 3 of 5 could get in from that group.
This time, the math changes. And so do the numbers Belichick brings.
Fox Sports reports Belichick's career record as a head coach, playoffs included, is 333 wins and 178 losses, second only to Don Shula's 347 victories.
He won 6 Super Bowls in New England, more than any other head coach in the Super Bowl era, and 2 more as a Giants assistant, for 8 rings as a coach, NBC News reports.
NBC called his New England tenure the "most successful 24-season run in NFL history."
Few coaches in any sport have a résumé like that. Which is why the snub stunned so many fans.
But the man next to him on the ballot has his own numbers.
Kraft has owned the Patriots since 1994, Fox Sports reports. In that time the team reached the Super Bowl 11 times, which Fox says is 3 more than any other owner, and won all 6 of its titles.
The Patriots have 20 division titles, 11 conference crowns and 36 playoff wins, the most in the league, Fox reports.
Kraft paid a then-record 172 million dollars for the franchise and kept it from moving to St.
Louis, according to Fox. He also privately financed Gillette Stadium and has served on 17 owners' committees.
If both men get in, the coach and the owner who built the dynasty would enter Canton together.
And they will not be alone on the ballot.
The players' list will be cut to 50 on November 24, 2026, Fox Sports reports.
In December it drops to 25, then to 13 players who join Belichick and Kraft as the 15 finalists.
The Boston Globe lists first-year eligible players including Rob Gronkowski, Ben Roethlisberger, Adrian Peterson, Richard Sherman, Cam Newton and Antonio Brown, with Eli Manning returning.
Gronkowski played for Belichick. Manning beat him in 2 Super Bowls. Only 3 to 7 will get in, and Belichick and Kraft each take up one of the 15 seats in that final room.
Every player who makes it to the final 13 will be competing for the same handful of spots, and every voter will have to decide whether a coach's 6 titles count more than a quarterback's or a running back's career.
That puts Belichick in a room with some of his former players and his old rivals.

Belichick started as an assistant with the Baltimore Colts in 1975, Fox Sports reports.
He joined the Giants in 1979, and Bill Parcells made him defensive coordinator in 1985, NBC News reports, a job he held through 2 Super Bowl wins.
He was the Browns' head coach from 1991 to 1995, with 1 playoff win.
Then came assistant jobs under Parcells in New England and with the Jets.
In 2000, he resigned as Jets head coach after 1 day and took the Patriots job, NBC reports.
Many of the men watching this remember every one of those stops.
What came next made him the most debated coach in football.
The cheating questions never fully went away. Spygate brought discipline from the league over videotaping signals, NBC News reports.
Deflategate brought another scandal years later. ESPN, as cited by NBC, linked both to the 2026 voting.
Supporters say his wins came over 24 seasons with many different rosters, and that no taping explains 6 titles.
Critics say the Hall of Fame is about more than winning. That argument will be in the room again in February, with fewer voters and the same 80 percent bar.
And Belichick has a day job that keeps him in the news.
Belichick is the head coach at North Carolina, hired in December 2024, NBC News reports.
His first season with the Tar Heels ended 4 and 8. He is 74 years old, and his last NFL job was in the 2023 season.
In his final 2 seasons in New England, the Patriots went 12 and 22.
For a coach who once seemed unbeatable, those are strange numbers. But Hall of Fame voters judge the whole career, not the last chapter.
And in that whole career, one number keeps coming back.
What does a yes actually mean? A new Hall of Famer receives the gold jacket, a bronze bust in Canton, Ohio, and a speech at the enshrinement ceremony.
The Boston Globe notes that neither Kraft nor Belichick earned a gold jacket in 2026.
For the Class of 2027, enshrinement in Canton comes in August 2027, Fox Sports reports.
Picture the scene if both get in: the owner who bought the team for 172 million dollars to keep it in New England, and the coach who turned it into a 6-time champion, on the same stage in the same summer.
For Patriots fans, it would close the book on the dynasty. For Belichick's critics, it would mean the voters decided the wins outweigh the scandals.
And for the 80 percent rule, it would mean at least 4 out of every 5 voters in the room agreed.
The ballot does not leave room for a partial answer. It is yes or no.
And many other famous names got a no in 2026 without even reaching the ballot.
Kraft's name has waited too. So has a long line of coaches who were considered by the coach committee in 2026.
Fox Sports lists Tom Coughlin, Mike Holmgren, Dan Reeves and Mike Shanahan among the coach candidates this cycle, and Doug Williams, Ralph Hay, John Wooten and Virginia McCaskey among the contributor candidates.
Only Belichick and Kraft moved forward. For fans who watched Reeves in Denver, Coughlin with the Giants and Shanahan with John Elway, those are familiar names that will wait at least another year.
Every name on that list has a case. Belichick's is simply the largest.
So here is where it stands. Bill Belichick and Robert Kraft are finalists for the Class of 2027, after both were passed over in February.
Each needs at least 80 percent of a selection committee that was cut from 50 to 28 members.
Next comes November 24, 2026, when the players' list is cut to 50.
In December, it shrinks to 25 and then to 13. The full committee meets before Super Bowl 61, and the class will be announced at NFL Honors in Los Angeles in February, Fox Sports reports.
Enshrinement in Canton follows in August 2027. For Belichick, it is a second chance at the honor many fans assumed was automatic.
Where it stands: two finalists, one vote, and a gold jacket still waiting.
