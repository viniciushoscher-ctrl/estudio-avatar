The man leading Ukraine says Washington wants this war over within weeks.
In an interview with Bloomberg News in Kyiv on November 23, 2026, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said President Trump's envoys, Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, want a peace deal with Russia "as soon as possible," ideally before January.
The price being floated is huge: two whole regions of eastern Ukraine.
In the next minutes: what is on the table, the 48 American missiles stuck waiting in Washington, and how the last three American-brokered peace deals actually ended.
According to Bloomberg, Zelenskyy said the American negotiators want to move fast.
"There's a desire to move very quickly, both we and the Russians understand that," he said, as quoted by European Pravda.
And then he added a line that tells you where Kyiv stands: "We very much want peace too, but not at Ukraine's expense alone."
Bloomberg reports Kushner and Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in Moscow and Zelenskyy in Dublin earlier in November 2026.
The first U.S.-led talks between Russia and Ukraine since February took place in Abu Dhabi.
And in mid-November, Bloomberg reports, Trump and Putin discussed Ukraine at talks in China.
So there were three capitals and two envoys working on one deadline.
That deadline is the first thing to understand.
European Pravda reports that, according to earlier media reports, the Trump administration told European and Ukrainian officials it wanted a deal before the beginning of January 2027.
That was roughly five weeks after the interview. Bloomberg's own reporting says Trump would need to convince Putin to end the war by the end of the year.
According to European Pravda, Kushner said on November 23 that U.S. negotiators would work with Ukraine, the European Union and Russia over the coming weeks on a common security architecture.
Zelenskyy, for his part, says Putin shows no sign of ending the war.
Five weeks is very little time. And the price on the table is the hardest part.
According to Bloomberg and Newsmax, Kushner and Witkoff have floated proposals under which Ukraine would give up the Luhansk and Donetsk regions, together known as the Donbas, in exchange for some NATO-backed security guarantees.
Russia already controls most of Luhansk. Putin wants the rest of Donetsk.
Ukraine rejects the idea. Kyiv says handing over that land would leave the rest of the country exposed to the next attack.
European Pravda reports Ukraine faces "significant concessions." So the question for Zelenskyy is simple and brutal: what would he get in return?
He told Bloomberg exactly what he wants.
Zelenskyy said allied talks have focused on security guarantees modeled on NATO's Article 5, the promise that an attack on one is an attack on all.
But Bloomberg reports he is skeptical about how such guarantees would work in practice.
He wants firm military and sanctions commitments, support for joining the European Union, and what Bloomberg describes as hundreds of billions of dollars for the military and for rebuilding the country.
He said full NATO membership would give stronger protection, according to Newsmax.
Putin has ruled that out. So the two sides were still far apart on the one thing that matters most: what stops Russia from coming back.
And at that moment, Ukraine was short on the weapon that stops Russian missiles.
According to Bloomberg and Newsmax, a delivery of 48 Patriot interceptor missiles is waiting for authorization from Secretary Pete Hegseth.
Newsmax reports that even that shipment would still fall short of Ukraine's needs.
Both outlets report the United States has supplied 12 Patriot interceptors a month since 2025.
That is about a quarter of the number promised under the Biden administration.
Zelenskyy asked allies to cover Ukraine's needs "for at least the whole of December," Bloomberg reports.
Bloomberg also reports restrictions remain on Ukraine's use of SpaceX's Starshield terminals in Russian-occupied areas.
Why does December matter so much? Because of what Russia was hitting that winter.
Bloomberg reports Russia is using mass drone and missile attacks against power plants, heating, water supply, and the bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv.
Newsmax reports Russia has broadened its strikes to water infrastructure. At the same time, Bloomberg says Russia's advance in Donetsk has stalled.
And Ukraine is hitting back. According to Bloomberg, Ukraine destroyed a section of road on the Crimean Bridge in mid-November 2026 and stepped up strikes on Russian oil refineries.
Zelenskyy told Bloomberg: "In 2026, Putin has completely changed the course of the war."
He did not mean it as good news. So if the deal does not come, what is Zelenskyy's plan B?
It runs through the U.S. Capitol.
According to Bloomberg, Zelenskyy said Ukraine would seek a robust American military aid package and tougher sanctions on Russia if no acceptable deal is reached.
He wants the new Congress, which takes office in January 2027 after Republicans lost sweeping midterm races, to press Russia.
Bloomberg reports he plans a "new approach to the new Congress in January or February."
In his words: "There must be such a level of support for Ukraine and such sanctions that they send a clear signal to the Russians."
And then: "It's time to sit down at the negotiating table and end the war."
No response from Moscow to the interview was reported by the outlets we read.
Americans have seen wars end at a negotiating table before, and the deal rarely ends the story.

Many of you remember Korea, or grew up with fathers and uncles who fought there.
According to Wikipedia, armistice talks began on July 10, 1951 at Kaesong, then moved to Panmunjom.
They went on for about two years. The armistice was finally signed on July 27, 1953.
It created a ceasefire line and a demilitarized zone 2.5 miles wide, and set up neutral nations to supervise it.
But no formal peace treaty was ever signed. South Korea never signed the armistice at all.
More than 70 years later, in 2026, the line was still there.
The talks that end a war can last longer than anyone expects.
The next lesson is about what a guarantee is worth.
This is where the Ukraine deal touches Americans directly. The guarantees being discussed are modeled on NATO's Article 5, according to Bloomberg.
That is the same promise that ties the United States to defend its allies.
Zelenskyy wants guarantees backed by firm military commitments, not just words on paper.
Kushner says the United States will work with Ukraine, Europe and Russia on what he calls a common security architecture.
The open question is who would enforce it, with what troops, and paid for by whom.
None of the reports we read gave those answers. And the next precedent shows what happens when a guarantee is not enforced.
The Paris Peace Accords were signed on January 27, 1973 at the Hotel Majestic in Paris, according to Wikipedia.
Talks had started in May 1968. That is nearly five years. Henry Kissinger had announced in the fall of 1972 that "peace is at hand."
The deal required all American forces to withdraw within 60 days and the return of prisoners of war, which brought our POWs home in Operation Homecoming.
But North Vietnamese troops were allowed to stay in the South. Fighting resumed within weeks.
On April 30, 1975, Saigon fell. For a Vietnam veteran watching Ukraine, the warning is in the fine print.
Which brings us to the one deal many experts call a success.
There is one big difference between those wars and this one. In Korea and in Vietnam, American troops were fighting on the ground.
In Ukraine, the United States supplies weapons, like the Patriot interceptors, but it has not sent combat troops.
That gives Washington leverage, but less control. Zelenskyy's demand for firm guarantees is really a demand to avoid a repeat of a deal that falls apart.
And the closest model to what the envoys are proposing, land divided in exchange for guarantees, was signed in Ohio.
In November 1995, the presidents of Bosnia, Croatia and Serbia were brought to Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio, according to Wikipedia.
The conference lasted about three weeks, from November 1 to November 21.
Richard Holbrooke led the negotiations. The deal split Bosnia into two entities, with the Bosnian Serbs holding about 49 percent of the land.
It was signed in Paris on December 14, 1995. NATO peacekeepers deployed six days later.
The guns went quiet, and the war did not restart. But European troops were still deployed there in 2026.
Dayton shows a deal can be done fast. It also shows someone has to stay and guard it.
Here is where we are. Zelenskyy says Trump's envoys want a deal before January.
The Donbas is on the table, and Ukraine rejects giving it up. 48 Patriot interceptors are waiting for Pentagon approval.
And if there is no acceptable deal, Zelenskyy plans to turn to the new Congress.
Watch three things. Whether the 48 Patriot interceptors are approved before December 2026 ends.
Whether Moscow accepts the January target. And the new Congress in January 2027, where Zelenskyy plans his next push for aid and sanctions.
Where it stands: no deal yet, the Donbas still disputed, and a January deadline that Moscow has not accepted.
