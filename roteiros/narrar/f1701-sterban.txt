Richard Sterban, the deep bass voice that sang "oom-papa-mow-mow" on "Elvira," died on November 14, 2026, in Hendersonville, Tennessee.
He was 83. He sang with the Oak Ridge Boys for more than 50 years, and before that, he sang behind Elvis Presley.
His family and the band said he died of complications from bronchitis and pneumonia after a long fight with cancer.
In the next minutes: how he spent his last months, including one last "Elvira" on the stage of the Grand Ole Opry, what happens to the Oak Ridge Boys after him, and the year and a half he spent on the road with the King.
The band posted the news on social media. "It is with heavy hearts that we announce the passing of our partner and brother, Richard Sterban."
His family thanked fans for their love, support and prayers, and asked them to "continue to lift us up in this difficult time."
They also said they take comfort knowing he is "face to face with Jesus."
Hendersonville sits just north of Nashville, on Old Hickory Lake. Generations of country singers have lived there, and it was home for Sterban for decades.
He leaves his wife, Donna, five children and 11 grandchildren and great-grandchildren.
Funeral arrangements were to be announced later, the band's website said. The Country Music Association posted its condolences.
But the hardest part of the story started months earlier, with a diagnosis he kept quiet at first.
Doctors diagnosed Sterban with pancreatic cancer in March of last year. He kept singing.
In May, his health forced him to step away from touring. Two other bass singers, Aaron Lee McCune and Tim Duncan, took his place on the road.
Fans wondered why the famous low notes on "Elvira" sounded different. In September, Sterban told them himself.
He said he had pancreatic cancer, and he sounded hopeful. "Most days I'm feeling very good," he said, "and I hope to be back out on the road in several months."
Pancreatic cancer is one of the hardest cancers to treat. He did not make it back on tour full time.
But he did make it back to the one stage that mattered most to a country singer.
On January 30 and 31 of this year, Richard Sterban walked back onto the stage of the Grand Ole Opry in Nashville and sang "Elvira" with his band.
The crowd knew what it was hearing. Lead singer Duane Allen said, "Having Richard on stage with us again was one of God's greatest blessings."
It was the song that made him famous, and the bass line everyone tried to copy in the car.
The Opry crowd stood. For two nights, the four-part harmony was whole again, with the original bass voice back where it belonged.
Those two nights were among his last performances with the group. The Oak Ridge Boys are members of the Opry.
And it turns out those two nights were not the last time his voice would reach the public.
There was one more record.
In October, just weeks before he died, the Oak Ridge Boys released an album called "Buried Treasures."
Sterban sang on several of its tracks. That makes it his last recorded work.
Last month, he also showed up at the Country Music Hall of Fame's Medallion Ceremony for the Class of 2026, where new members are honored.
Photos from that night show him smiling, three weeks before his death.
The Hall of Fame's chief executive, Kyle Young, called him "a steady force and a foundational figure in country music."
For a man who spent more than 50 years in one band, it was a long goodbye in public.
He did not hide from cameras, and he did not stop showing up.
And it leaves a hard question for the fans: who is left?
For most fans over 60, the Oak Ridge Boys are four men: Duane Allen on lead, Joe Bonsall on tenor, William Lee Golden on baritone, and Richard Sterban on bass.
That is the lineup that sang "Elvira" and "Bobbie Sue." Joe Bonsall died two years ago, at 76, of complications from ALS.
Sterban is gone. That leaves Duane Allen and William Lee Golden, the two men who were already in the group before Sterban walked in.
PennLive put it in one line: "Only two left." Bonsall and Sterban had been friends long before the Oak Ridge Boys.
They started a gospel group together as young men in New Jersey.
And Sterban's road to Nashville took a turn through Graceland first.
The Country Music Hall of Fame called him "a musical bridge builder."
Kyle Young credited the Oak Ridge Boys with "seventeen No. 1 hits."
The Country Music Association posted condolences. Fans filled the band's social media pages with memories of concerts, county fairs and church services.
Many wrote the same four words: "oom-papa-mow-mow." Sterban was born on April 24, 1943, in Camden, New Jersey, and grew up in Collingswood.
He studied music at Trenton State College, now the College of New Jersey.
A kid from South Jersey ended up as one of the best-known bass voices in country music.
And before Nashville, there was Elvis.
Before the Oak Ridge Boys, Sterban toured with J.D. Sumner and the Stamps Quartet, the gospel group that backed Elvis Presley on stage.
He sang behind Elvis for about a year and a half, the Los Angeles Times reports.
He is in the 1972 concert film "Elvis on Tour," the documentary that won a Golden Globe.
Then, in late 1972, he made a decision most singers would not make.
He left the King's band to join the Oak Ridge Boys, then a gospel quartet.
"I wanted to be in the best vocal group in the world," he said.
It took nine years for that choice to pay off, and when it did, it paid off with one song.
The band he chose has to go on without him.
The Oak Ridge Boys have kept performing through loss. When Bonsall left the road, Ben James joined on tenor, and the group set aside retirement plans it had once announced.
Since last year, McCune and Duncan have handled the bass parts on tour.
The band has not announced any changes to its schedule since Sterban's death, and its website says funeral arrangements are still to come.
On September 19, 2023, the group had called its tour a farewell.
After Ben James arrived, that retirement was called off, and the Oak Ridge Boys kept booking dates.
That decision means the harmony goes on, even as the men who built it leave one by one.
Duane Allen and William Lee Golden have outlasted two of their brothers.
For the band's fans, the songs are still on the radio. And the song that made them famous was not even new when they recorded it.
